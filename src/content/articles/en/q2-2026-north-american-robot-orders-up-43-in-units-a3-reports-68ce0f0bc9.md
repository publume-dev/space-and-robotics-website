- In Q2 2026, North American companies ordered 8,940 robots valued at $622 million, a 4.3% increase in units and a 21.3% increase in revenue compared to Q2 2025.
- First-half 2026 robot orders totaled 17,995 units valued at $1.166 billion, representing 2.0% growth in units and 6.6% growth in order value over the first half of 2025.
- Automotive OEM orders declined 25% in the first half of 2026 compared to 2025, while growth in other sectors offset the decline: semiconductors/electronics/photonics +35%, life sciences/pharma/biomedical +32%, automotive components +24%, food and consumer goods +17%, plastics and rubber +6%, all other industries +6%, metals and metalworking +3% (units).
- In Q2 2026, non-automotive customers accounted for 56% of robot units ordered, reflecting continued diversification of demand.
- Collaborative robots accounted for 2,774 units (15.4% of all units) and $114 million (9.8% of revenue) in first-half 2026 orders; in Q2 alone, 1,137 cobots were ordered valued at $44 million, representing 12.7% of units and 7.1% of revenue.
- Collaborative robot adoption was particularly strong in healthcare (43.7% of first-half orders) and electronics (36.5%).
- A3 stated that manufacturing PMI remained in expansion territory for a sixth consecutive month in June, and Federal Reserve data showed manufacturing output 1.1% above year-earlier levels.
