- NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the Aug. 7 Idaho National Laboratory tour increased his confidence in launching SR-1 Freedom by the end of 2028.
- SR-1 Freedom is slated for late-2028 launch, using a 20-kilowatt-electric reactor to power an electric propulsion system originally developed for lunar Gateway, and is intended to deploy SkyFall helicopters at Mars.
- The reactor is based on INL's VALKRE concept and will use high-assay low-enriched uranium supplied by the Department of Energy.
- NASA has a preliminary cost estimate of $2.1 billion for SR-1 Freedom.
- NASA recently completed a design sync review for SR-1 Freedom and was preparing a contract modification to Intuitive Machines for needed modifications to the Power and Propulsion Element.
- Reactor design details and VALKRE hardware test status were not disclosed; NASA and INL officials did not provide technical specifics during the visit.

- Next milestone: to meet the December 2028 launch date, the SR-1 Freedom reactor must be completed by spring 2028; DOE's national technical director identified component procurement as the biggest schedule challenge.
