- The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope launched at 7:26 a.m. EDT on Sunday, August 30, 2026, aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket from Kennedy Space Center.
- Roman is now on a three-month, million-mile journey to the Sun-Earth L2 Lagrange point.
- The mission will survey the infrared sky to study dark matter, dark energy, and exoplanets.
- About 70 minutes after launch, the Deep Space Network took over communications, and solar panels and lower instrument sun shade deployed successfully 1 hour and 23 minutes after launch.
- Within the upcoming days, Roman's high-gain antenna and deployable aperture cover will deploy, and the Coronagraph Instrument will power on.
- Roman's primary instrument, the Wide Field Instrument, is a 300-megapixel infrared camera with 18 4K detectors, and is expected to activate a few weeks into the voyage.
- The Roman telescope is designed to survey the universe a thousand times faster than NASA's Hubble Space Telescope.
- NASA anticipates releasing Roman's first images by early 2027.
