- ESA's Cluster satellites Samba and Tango will reenter Earth's atmosphere on 31 August and 1 September respectively, with targeted descents over a remote area of the South Pacific Ocean.
- ESA is conducting an airborne observation campaign from Tonga, using 30 instruments including spectral sensors and cameras to collect reentry data.
- Observations from the previous Salsa reentry revealed a mismatch of up to 20% with expected atmospheric density and earlier-than-predicted break-up, indicating uncertainties in reentry forecasting.
- During the penultimate perigee of the first reentering Cluster satellite, the spacecraft remained intact and healthy at an altitude of about 110 km traveling at more than 10 km/s, making them the lowest-flying active spacecraft ever.
