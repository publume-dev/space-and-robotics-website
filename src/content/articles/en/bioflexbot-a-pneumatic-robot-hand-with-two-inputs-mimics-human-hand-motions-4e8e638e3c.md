- Researchers developed BioflexBot, a robot hand that mimics key human hand motions using a coiled spring, constraining shell, and pneumatic system, controlled by just two pneumatic inputs.
- Validated capabilities include: pinching (manipulating an acupuncture needle, using a pipette), rotating a bottle cap almost four times more than a human hand, hooking objects like toolbox and goggles, and grasping objects up to almost 13 times bigger than similar systems.
- It extends and contracts 3.5 times more than a human hand, enabling grasping complex objects, reaching long distances, and transporting objects in confined spaces.
- Three potential applications were demonstrated: inspecting aeroengine blades, completing daily tasks when integrated with a humanoid robot, and conducting a chemistry experiment.

If independently verified, the design could extend robot hand applications in fine manipulation and extended reach tasks, such as industrial inspection and daily assistance.

Watch for the formal publication of the study in the journal Advanced Science for methodology details and independent validation.
