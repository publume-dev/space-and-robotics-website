- Virgin Galactic now expects commercial flights of its first Spaceship suborbital vehicle to begin in February 2027, a delay from the previous expectation of the fourth quarter of 2026.
- The delay is due to many small assembly issues, not a single major problem, including components fitting a few thousandths of an inch off, requiring extra reviews.
- Integrated vehicle ground tests are expected to begin later in August 2026, and the vehicle is scheduled to move to Spaceport America in October 2026 for flight tests.
- Virgin Galactic ended the second quarter of 2026 with $286 million in cash and cash equivalents, sufficient to reach commercial service without additional capital.
- The company closed a ticket tranche at $750,000 each, raising more than $50 million from oversubscribed sales, and plans to open the next tranche at a higher price later in 2026.
