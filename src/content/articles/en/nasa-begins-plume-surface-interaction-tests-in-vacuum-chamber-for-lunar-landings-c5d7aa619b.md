- NASA Langley has begun plume-surface interaction tests inside a 60-foot spherical vacuum chamber to study lunar lander engine plume effects on regolith.
- The first round uses an ethane plume simulation system generating about 100 pounds of thrust, firing into a bin of simulated lunar regolith (Black Point-1).
- Instruments include a version of the Stereo Cameras for Lunar Plume Surface Studies system used during Firefly's Blue Ghost Mission-1 landing in 2025.
- Later this year, a second round of tests will use a 14-inch 3D-printed hybrid rocket motor developed by Utah State University, producing about 35 pounds of thrust.
- The test campaign is modular and can be adapted for Mars by replacing the regolith simulant and adjusting chamber pressure.
