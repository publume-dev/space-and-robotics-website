- Hubble Space Telescope observations revealed a 10-sided atmospheric wave (decagon) encircling Saturn's south pole, with results published in Science Advances.
- This is the first large, regular-sided jet pattern observed in Saturn's southern hemisphere, distinct from the northern hexagon.
- The decagon was first noticed in ground-based images from 2024 by Agustín Sánchez-Lavega and amateur astronomers, with Hubble data confirming it back to 2023.
- The wave sits within a jet stream and extends through multiple atmospheric layers, indicating vertical extension rather than a cloud-level feature only.
- The team plans continued Hubble and James Webb Space Telescope observations plus computer modeling to determine the decagon's formation, duration, and link to the northern hexagon.

The finding may influence understanding of Saturn's polar meteorology and polygon waves driven by jet instabilities in similar gas giants, though its full impact on long-term planetary dynamics theories remains to be assessed.

Ongoing observations with Hubble and Webb, along with computer modeling, will determine whether the decagon persists similarly to the northern hexagon or is short-lived.
