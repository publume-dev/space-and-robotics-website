- A 76-square-kilometer section of Petermann Glacier's floating ice tongue calved on 4 August 2026, the largest loss since 2012.
- The calving was detected using Sentinel-1 radar imagery, which operates day and night and penetrates cloud cover.
- The Sentinel-1C and 1D tandem phase provided one-day repeat synthetic aperture radar images to measure fracture propagation.
- Two further large ice islands, with estimated areas of 97 and 87 square kilometers, could detach as existing rifts propagate.

Beyond the immediate event, this calving could influence predictions of sea-level rise and highlights the critical role of radar satellites in polar monitoring.

The research team will continue to monitor the glacier and iceberg using satellite imagery, aerial observations, and tracking data.
