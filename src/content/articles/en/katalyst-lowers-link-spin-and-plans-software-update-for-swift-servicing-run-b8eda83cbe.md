- Link’s spin rate was cut from about 9 degrees per second to 1.47 degrees per second using one electric thruster, consuming less than 100 grams of fuel.
- Two of Link’s three reaction wheels were not functioning after the attitude-control incident, and its reaction control system thrusters were only partially working.
- Katalyst planned to upload a significant flight-software update to enable attitude control in the spacecraft’s current configuration, restoring full attitude control and resuming plans to reach NASA’s Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory.
- The incident’s cause was not disclosed, and Link’s remaining propellant mass was not disclosed; Katalyst said it is conserving propellant for later mission phases.

- Without Link, Swift is expected to deorbit late this year or early next year; the reboost must start before Swift reaches an altitude of about 300 kilometers, a threshold it is expected to cross no earlier than October.

- Current plans call for Katalyst to reach Swift in late August, a few weeks later than originally planned, to attempt grappling and reboosting the observatory’s orbit.
