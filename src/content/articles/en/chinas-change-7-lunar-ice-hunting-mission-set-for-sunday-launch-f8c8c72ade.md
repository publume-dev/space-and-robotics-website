- China will launch Chang'e-7 on a Long March 5 rocket from Wenchang Satellite Launch Center on Hainan island, with the launch window opening around 8:00 p.m. Eastern on Aug 23 (0000 UTC, Aug. 24).
- The mission consists of an orbiter, a lander, a rover, and a hopper spacecraft, targeting the lunar south pole to search for water-ice in shadowed craters.
- The lander will attempt a precise landing with a sub-100 meter ellipse, targeting a feature known as Peak Near Shackleton near the Shackleton crater.
- The hopper will fly into shadowed craters, use its Lunar soil Water molecule Analyser (LUWA) to scan for frost, drill up to roughly a meter deep to retrieve samples, and analyze them in a sealed heating chamber with mass and laser spectrometers.
- The orbiter, lander, rover, and Queqiao-2 relay satellite carry a range of science payloads including a seismograph, cameras, magnetometers, spectrometers, and a lunar penetrating radar.
- Chang'e-7 is intended to lay the technical groundwork for China's planned International Lunar Research Station (ILRS), with the follow-on Chang'e-8 mission planned for around 2029.
