- Four Aerospace Corp. DiskSats were deployed from a custom-built dispenser to 550 km after a December 2025 Rocket Lab Electron launch from Wallops, with each 17-kg satellite descending about 2 km per month due to drag.
- The DiskSat bus is 1 m in diameter and 2.5 cm deep, built with carbon fiber composite facesheets bonded to an aluminum-honeycomb core, requiring redesign of power, communications, attitude control, and thermal subsystems.
- Engineers mitigated a battery heater design flaw that drew power unequally through software; before implementation, both batteries on DiskSat C and one on DiskSat A were permanently disabled.
- Aerospace engineers remedied a stray-light issue affecting star trackers by revising the DiskSat concept of operations.
- The mission is on track to complete all mission objectives, with the next phase being propulsive lowering and sustainment at VLEO below 300 km using Enpulsion Nano Field Emission Electric Propulsion thrusters.

Neumann Space, Orbotic Systems, and Satlyt have signed commercial licensing agreements for DiskSat technology, with additional companies expressing interest.
