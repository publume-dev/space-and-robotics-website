- LRO captured images of a new crater on the Moon formed on Aug. 5 when a SpaceX Falcon 9 upper stage impacted the surface, following its January 2025 launch of the Firefly Blue Ghost 1 mission.
- Engineers tilted LRO's cameras toward the crater as it passed about 60 miles above the Moon, traveling 1 mile per second, and the spacecraft had to wait six days for the target to come into view.
- Scientists measured the crater's width at 60 feet and determined it is less than 10 feet deep based on shadow length, using the Narrow-Angle Camera which can spot features as small as 3 feet wide.
- The images show bright and dark rays; darker streaks are weathered surface material excavated from 1.5 feet deep, while brighter streaks are fresh material from deeper underground.
- NASA's CNEOS refined the trajectory and provided the impact location, which the Danuri team imaged with the LUTI camera, confirming the prediction was accurate to about 0.6 miles.
- Comparing new and pre-impact images, the LRO team updated the crater center coordinates to 19.4759°N, 266.7138°E, 511 meters elevation.
