- SpaceNews reports that Japan’s H3-22S rocket successfully launched the Michibiki 7 navigation satellite on Aug. 10, deploying it into a planned transfer orbit 29 minutes after liftoff.
- Michibiki 7 is a 4.9-metric-ton QZSS satellite meant to operate in a slightly inclined geosynchronous orbit to augment GPS coverage over Japan.
- The launch was the first H3-22S flight since the December 2025 failure of Michibiki 5, which was attributed to payload fairing separation shocks that damaged the satellite and adapter, causing an upper-stage liquid hydrogen pressure loss.
- The H3 had returned to flight in June with the H3-30S configuration carrying small satellites.
- Japanese national policy aims for 30 launches per year from Japan by the early 2030s; MHI and JAXA are discussing raising H3 launch capacity from six to eight per year.
- The report does not clarify how many QZSS satellites are currently operational after this launch, since Michibiki 5 was lost and the status of Michibiki 6 is not mentioned; it also does not state whether the system now meets the five-satellite requirement for standalone positioning.

Watch: An H3 launch is projected for November for JAXA’s MMX mission to the moons of Mars, and the second HTV-X cargo mission is tentatively scheduled for later this year.
