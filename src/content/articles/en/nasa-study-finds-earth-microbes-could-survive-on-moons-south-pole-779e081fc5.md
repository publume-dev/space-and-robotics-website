- A NASA-led study found that some Earth microbes, including Aspergillus niger and Bacillus subtilis, could survive in shaded regions of the Moon's South Pole for at least one Earth day.
- Using Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter data and radiation models, the study identified survivable niches ranging from a miles-wide crater floor to an astronaut's boot print in regions Nobile Rim, Connecting Ridge, and De Gerlache Rim.
- The findings highlight that microbial contamination from astronauts could complicate the search for signs of life on Mars, emphasizing the need for baseline contamination measurements before crewed missions.
- The article does not specify the exact survival rates or comparative resistance of each tested microbe beyond noting Aspergillus niger's resistance to UV radiation.
- The claim about the microbes surviving is based on a single NASA article; the original peer-reviewed paper is not directly accessible, though the primary source is authoritative.
