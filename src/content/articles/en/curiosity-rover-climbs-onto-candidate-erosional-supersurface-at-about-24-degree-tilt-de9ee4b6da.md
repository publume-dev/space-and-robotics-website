- NASA's Curiosity rover climbed onto a candidate erosional supersurface in Gale crater during Sols 4968-4974, finishing at about a 24-degree tilt, close to the mission's contact-science tilt record of 27 degrees.
- Mastcam captured large stereo mosaics of the Cerro Paine Grande vertical exposure just below the candidate supersurface, and a 360-degree panorama after the rover climbed on top.
- Contact science in the Sol 4968 workspace included co-targeting "Puyehue" with APXS, MAHLI, and ChemCam LIBS; LIBS observations at "Lago Palena" and "Piedras Juntas"; and an APXS measurement of the sand target "Cormudesi".
- In the Sol 4972 workspace, MAHLI, APXS, and LIBS measured the light-toned top surface at "Sierra de Sangre"; APXS and MAHLI targeted the darker-toned laminated face at "Laguna del Laja"; and a MAHLI mosaic "Longquimay" documented fine-scale sedimentary structures.
- The science team is investigating the candidate supersurface to understand Gale crater's shift from net deposition to net erosion and back, with patterns suggesting aeolian features and possible fluvial "lens" deposits.

Next milestone: higher-resolution imaging of the candidate supersurface to resolve aeolian features and possible fluvial "lens" deposits that current images cannot distinguish.
