- Landspace completed a successful vertical landing of its Zhuque-3 first stage on Aug. 18, 2026, becoming the third entity worldwide after SpaceX and Blue Origin to land an orbital-class booster on legs and the first Chinese company to recover a booster on land.
- Landspace plans to conduct a reflight test of the recovered booster within six months, according to a company representative.
- The landing occurred eight and a half months after the debut flight of Zhuque-3, which reached orbit but failed its first stage recovery attempt.
- Technical changes after the failed first landing attempt included reducing the number of engines used in the landing burn, adding a predicted landing point to the autonomous safety control system, and improving thermal protection.
- The second flight also carried China Mobile's Space-Based Intelligent Computing Payload 1.0, marking China Mobile's first payload aimed at distributed edge computing in orbit.
- Landspace's design target calls for a Zhuque-3 first stage to be reusable at least 20 times.
