- The Pentagon announced multibillion-dollar agreements with Lockheed Martin and Northrop Grumman to increase production of PAC-3 MSE and THAAD interceptors and components, following a $1 billion investment in L3Harris for solid rocket motor capacity.
- Northrop Grumman's PAC-3 arrangement is a seven-year framework worth roughly $2 billion to supply PAC-3 MSE solid rocket motors as a newly qualified second source and increase production of ignition-safety devices.
- Under a separate agreement valued at about $1 billion, Northrop will quadruple production of THAAD interceptor shell structures, aft bulkhead assemblies, and heat shields, supplied exclusively to Lockheed Martin.
- The Pentagon and Lockheed Martin aim to raise annual THAAD production capacity from 96 to 400 interceptors.
- Army Lt. Gen. John Rafferty said the future of missile defense requires a mix of high-end and lower-cost interceptors, with technologies like lasers still needing to be proven.
- According to Tom Karako of CSIS, U.S. missile-defense inventories are at "dire" levels and solid rocket motors remain a production chokepoint.
