- Blue Origin will add a second pad, LC-36B, at Launch Complex 36 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, designed for the larger New Glenn 9x4 vehicle.
- The new pad will include a vertical integration facility and a payload processing facility; the Space Force awarded Blue Origin a $78.2 million contract in October 2025 for the payload processing facility.
- The rebuilt LC-36A will be used for the current New Glenn 7x2, which uses seven BE-4 and two BE-3U engines, while the New Glenn 9x4 will use nine BE-4 and four BE-3U engines.
- Blue Origin is testing a 'horizontal/vertical hybrid CONOPS' at LC-11, using cranes to stack an upper stage simulator and payload fairing on a forward dome, gathering wind-damping data and testing new mechanisms.

The new pad and hybrid CONOPS could increase launch cadence and support larger payloads, but the actual impact depends on construction progress and the maturity of the 9x4 vehicle.

Watch for Blue Origin to announce a timeline for LC-36B construction and a first flight date for New Glenn 9x4.
